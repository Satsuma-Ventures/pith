# Pith

**Scope:** Studio — thought leadership content development
**Tool:** Claude Chat (interview extraction); Claude Code (content bank, file management)
**Invoked:** On-demand — when developing a new Pith piece or capturing surplus thinking
**Version:** 1.8

---

## Purpose

Pith is Satsuma's thought leadership publication. Each piece is a long-form argument — declarative, not conversational — drawn from the founder's direct experience and perspective on AI, empowerment, and what it means to build well during a period of rapid technological change.

This skill governs the process for developing Pith content: how to extract thinking, how to capture surplus for future use, how to shape raw material into publishable pieces, and how to maintain the content bank over time.

---

## Audience

Every Pith piece is written for a specific cross-section: someone literate enough in tech and industry discourse to read a newsletter like TLDR comfortably, crossed with someone who will eventually use a Satsuma product and has no reason to know AI terminology.

This means insider language is fine — even valuable, for gravitas and credibility — but the argument itself must never depend on jargon to hold together. A reader who doesn't know what "a model" means, technically, should still follow the whole piece without friction. Prefer "AI" or "the technology" over "the model" unless the specific technical term is the actual subject of the sentence. The test: would this sentence still make sense to someone who has used ChatGPT twice and nothing else?

## Piece Types

Pith produces two distinct categories of piece. Both share the interview-based extraction process, the "Dictated, then shaped" attribution, and the studio's voice — but they differ in origin, structure, and purpose.

**Opinion pieces** — the default. Long-form, declarative, one claim made well. Drawn from open-ended interview sessions, not tied to a specific studio event. *Why We Build* is the first of these, and most of the pipeline in `content-bank.md` is opinion pieces.

**Venture Introductions** — tied to a specific studio event: a venture reaching Phase 7 (Ship) and opening to a broader audience. One per venture, timed around beta launch. Distinct from opinion pieces in three ways:

- **Occasion-driven, not open-ended.** The piece exists because the venture is going live, not because an idea has been fully worked through in advance.
- **Applied, not abstract.** It should show *Why We Build*'s thesis in action for one specific problem and one specific product — demonstrate the manifesto, don't restate it.
- **Includes an invitation.** Every other Pith piece is opinion-only, no ask. A Venture Introduction can close with a genuine invitation — join the beta, try the product, share it with someone who has the problem. This is the one place in Pith where a call to action belongs.

### Venture Introduction — interview protocol

A lighter version of the standard extraction process, scoped to the specific venture:

1. **The problem, plainly.** What's broken for the person this venture serves? Not the pitch-deck version — the plain one.
2. **Why a product, not an explanation.** Why does this deserve to exist as something people use, rather than something people are told?
3. **Why this, personally.** Not the business case for why this venture makes sense — the personal one. What connects Matt to the person this venture serves? A moment from his own life or work that made this problem real to him? This question is never skipped, even under launch pressure — it's the thread that makes the introduction feel like it came from a person, not a company.
4. **What building it taught you.** Something specific — a design decision, a piece of feedback already received, a moment the thesis got sharper or got challenged.
5. **Why now.** Why beta, why this venture is ready today — not a generic "why AI now" (that's *Why We Build*'s job), but why this specific thing, this specific moment.
6. **The invitation.** What do you actually want the reader to do? Try it? Share it with someone specific? Give honest feedback?

Every Venture Introduction should reference *Why We Build* — a link, a callback line, something that ties the specific venture back to the studio's founding argument. The venture is evidence; the manifesto is the claim.

## Guiding principles

**Interview over writing.** The founder doesn't write from a blank page. Structured interview extracts the thinking; the AI shapes it into narrative. This produces more honest, more specific, and more original content than starting with an outline.

**Personally grounded, always.** Every piece — opinion or Venture Introduction — has to connect back to why it matters to Matt personally, not just why it's true or why it's a good opportunity. Opinion pieces get this naturally through the Specificity phase of the interview. Venture Introductions don't get it for free — it has to be asked directly, every time, and it's never optional for the sake of moving faster toward launch.

**Extract more than you use.** A good interview session produces 3-5 pieces worth of material. Capture everything — the tangents, the half-formed ideas, the things that don't fit the current piece. That surplus is the content bank.

**Date everything.** This is a fast-moving moment. Perspectives evolve. Claims that were true in January may need revalidation by July. Every file, every entry, every piece carries a creation date. Stale content isn't discarded — it's flagged for revalidation before use.

**Declarative, not conversational.** Pith pieces make arguments. They don't ask questions or open discussions. The LinkedIn posts were community-building; Pith is point-of-view establishment.

**One argument per piece.** A Pith piece is not a survey of a topic. It's one claim, made well, with enough specificity that someone could disagree with it.

## Content bank staleness guidance

| Age | Status | Action before use |
|---|---|---|
| < 30 days | Current | Use freely |
| 30–90 days | Likely valid | Spot-check against recent developments |
| 90–180 days | Aging | Revalidate with founder before incorporating |
| > 180 days | Historical | Treat as prior POV — may have evolved; requires explicit founder reconfirmation |

AI and the broader technology landscape move fast enough that a perspective captured six months ago may need significant revision. The date is not a quality indicator — it's a freshness indicator.

## The extraction process

### Phase 1 — Foundation (what you believe)
Questions that establish the core POV on the topic. Aimed at surfacing the claim — the thing the founder actually thinks, not the thing that sounds reasonable.

Example questions:
- When you look at [X] right now, what's the most common mistake you see being made?
- What's the conventional wisdom on this that you think is just wrong?
- What would you say to someone who believes [opposite position]?

### Phase 2 — Specificity (where it comes from)
Questions that ground the POV in direct experience. The goal is to replace "I think" with "I saw" and "I did."

Example questions:
- What moment crystallized this for you?
- What does this look like in practice — concretely, not in principle?
- What have you tried that didn't work, and what did that teach you?

### Phase 3 — Stakes (why it matters)
Questions that establish why the argument matters beyond the founder's own experience.

Example questions:
- Who gets hurt if this goes wrong?
- What does this look like in five years if it isn't addressed?
- What does the better version of this look like?

### Phase 4 — Shape (editorial)
Closing questions that surface the emotional core and the provocation.

Example questions:
- What's the one thing you most want someone to feel after reading this — not think, feel?
- What's the thing you've said here that someone smart might push back on?
- What's the sentence you'd put on a poster?

## Interview mechanics

**One question at a time.** Never stack questions. Ask one, let the founder answer fully, then decide what to pull on next.

**Reflect and validate before pushing further.** After a substantive answer, reflect the key insight back in condensed form and ask the founder to confirm or correct before going deeper. This prevents compounding misreadings.

**Editorialize as suggestion, not assumption.** When making a connection or drawing an inference, flag it explicitly: "This might suggest X — does that resonate?" Not: "So what you're saying is X."

**Follow the energy.** If the founder pushes back, correct course immediately and note what the correction reveals. The corrections are often more useful than the original answer.

**The brain dump is valid input.** Run-on, unstructured, stream-of-consciousness answers are fine. The extraction process handles that. The founder shouldn't write well — they should think honestly.

## Shaping a piece

After the interview, the shaping process:

1. **Read the full transcript** — identify the 3-5 strongest moments (clearest claim, best specific example, most original observation)
2. **Name the argument** — one sentence, as specific as possible
3. **Build the architecture** — typically three movements: the tension, the inversion, the resolution
4. **Draft from the transcript** — not transcribing, but finding the narrative thread. The founder's voice should be present; it shouldn't sound like AI wrote it
5. **Return for validation** — share the draft with the founder before finalizing. Flag any place where the shaping diverged from the intent

## Content bank process

After each interview session, before drafting:

1. **Extract surplus insights** — anything that doesn't fit the current piece but is worth keeping
2. **Tag each insight** — by theme (see below) and by origin (which piece session, which phase)
3. **Date each entry** — to the session date
4. **Log the piece ideas** — any distinct pieces visible from this session's material

**Standard tags:**
`#empowerment` `#packaging` `#process` `#enterprise-AI` `#work-and-meaning` `#democratization` `#strategy-execution` `#AI-adoption` `#organizational-clarity` `#human-AI-collaboration`

## Numbering

Every published piece gets a permanent, sequential identifier: **Pith-001**, **Pith-002**, and so on — assigned in publish order, not draft order. A piece sitting in `content-bank.md`'s pipeline as an idea or an in-progress draft has no number yet. The number is claimed only when the piece actually publishes.

**Versioning follows standard semver logic, split across two eras:**

- **Pre-publish (drafting):** version starts at **v0.1** and increments with each meaningful draft revision — not every small wording tweak, but each version substantive enough to warrant a saved snapshot. Draft files carry no Pith number yet, since the piece hasn't published.
- **Publish:** the first published version is always **v1.0**, regardless of how many v0.x drafts preceded it. Draft iteration count is never exposed in the published version number.
- **Post-publish:** a minor bump (**v1.1**, **v1.2**...) for small edits — a correction, an added cross-reference to a later piece, a tightened sentence. A major bump (**v2.0**) for a substantial rewrite or restatement of the argument.

**Filing convention:** each piece lives in its own folder, and which top-level directory it sits under signals its stage — no need to open a folder to know whether something is still in progress or already public.

- **While drafting:** `pith/drafts/tbdnumber-[slug]/` — no Pith number yet, since it hasn't published. Contains `transcript-[date].md` and `draft-[date]-v0.X.md` files.
- **At publish:** move the whole folder to `pith/published/[NNN]-[slug]/`, renaming `tbdnumber` to the claimed number. Delete the `draft-[date]-v0.X.md` files — they're superseded. Keep `transcript-[date].md`; it moves with the piece. Add the actual published file: `pith-[NNN]-[slug]-v[version].md`.
- **Post-publish revisions:** each version bump adds a new `pith-[NNN]-[slug]-v[version].md` inside the same published folder, rather than overwriting the previous one — this preserves a clean history of every published revision.

Published title format (inside the piece itself): **Pith-[NNN] | [Title] v[version]**

The running index lives in `content-bank.md` under **Published Index**, updated every time a piece publishes, so the next number is always obvious at a glance.

## File structure

Pith is its **own repo** (`github.com/matterstling/pith` → https://pith.satsumaventures.com), separate from studio — this skill (the methodology), the content, and the published site all live there together:

```
pith/  (its own repo)
  pith.md                      ← this skill — the methodology (interview, extraction, shaping)
  build.js                     ← generates the published site from published/ (see PUBLISHING.md)
  PUBLISHING.md                ← the publish standard the build enforces
  content-bank.md              ← dated surplus insights, tagged by theme, Published Index at top
  drafts/
    tbdnumber-[slug]/           ← one folder per in-progress piece, no number yet
      transcript-[date].md      ← raw interview, lightly edited for readability
      insights-[date].md        ← extracted, organized thinking from this piece
      draft-[date]-v0.X.md      ← piece in progress
  published/
    [NNN]-[slug]/                          ← folder renamed from tbdnumber-[slug] at publish time
      transcript-[date].md                 ← moved here from drafts/, unchanged
      pith-[NNN]-[slug]-v[version].md      ← the published piece — new file per version bump
```

Stage lives at the top level (`drafts/` vs `published/`), so scanning the folder tree alone tells you what's in progress and what's live — no need to open individual pieces to check.

## Publishing

Only files under `published/[NNN]-[slug]/` are published, and only the highest version of each piece — the full standard the build enforces is in the pith repo's `PUBLISHING.md`. The essentials:

- The piece's H1 must be `# Pith-[NNN] | [Title] v[X.Y]`, followed by an italic byline line. These are parsed for the site; they must agree with the folder and filename.
- The slug becomes the URL: `published/001-why-we-build/` → `https://pith.satsumaventures.com/why-we-build/`.
- The **Published Index** table in `content-bank.md` is the source of truth for publish dates; update it in the same commit that publishes a piece.

**Cross-linking (WordPress-like).** Pieces link to each other with wiki-links in the markdown body, resolved at build time to the right URL and title:

- `[[why-we-build]]` — by slug · `[[001]]` or `[[pith-001]]` — by number · `[[why-we-build|custom text]]` — custom anchor.
- Links survive renumbering. An unresolved link is left visible as `[[…]]` and printed as a build warning.

The content-bank's `Links back to:` / `Links forward to:` notes are the planning layer; the `[[…]]` tokens are how those intentions become real hyperlinks in the published prose.

**Deploy.** Push to `main`; GitHub Actions runs `build.js` and deploys to Pages. Publishing a piece = add its folder under `published/`, update the Published Index, ensure `npm run build` is warning-free, commit, push.
