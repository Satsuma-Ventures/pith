**T-shaped (broad shallow knowledge, one deep specialty) is the old model of professional value. AI is making depth cheap and accessible in any single domain, which means the differentiator shifts to something "fence-shaped" — broad, and with the ability to go deep almost anywhere, not just one place. The more interesting version of this isn't just functional (able to go deep in any skill), it's industry-crossing (able to go deep in any domain) — which is the more Satsuma-oriented framing versus a narrower Zavari-oriented one about functional versatility.**
### Fence-Shaped, Not T-Shaped (working title)
*Captured: 2026-07-13 · From: Session 1, post-draft addition*
**Key insight to build from:** Matt's framing, verbatim: "Fence-shaped is the new T-shaped."
**Status:** `idea`
**Links to:** The Altitude Collapse / Clarity Tax cluster (functional version is more Zavari); Why We Build (industry-crossing version is more Satsuma — connects to the "negative knowledge base built from exposure across industries" idea already in that piece)
**External validation:** See External References below — The Skip's Stripe PM interview (Kevin Yien), "deep generalist" concept and the Meta economist/physicist PM example.

**"We tried that before and it didn't work" is treated as institutional wisdom, but very little of it comes with a real understanding of *why* it didn't work — most negative know-how is pattern-matched folklore, not diagnosed failure. AI creates an opportunity to actually interrogate those failures with enough rigor to know whether the "no-how" was ever real, and to challenge inherited assumptions that were never actually true.**
### Negative Know-How (working title)
*Captured: 2026-07-13 · From: Session 1, post-draft addition*
**Key insight to build from:** Matt's framing, verbatim: "How much of 'we tried that before and it didn't work' is real, and how much of it was truly understanding why it didn't work?"
**Status:** `idea`
**Links directly to:** The Design Sense Question — the "negative knowledge base" concept in Why We Build is presented there as a personal asset (Matt's pattern recognition from exposure). This piece complicates that idea from the other direction: most organizational negative know-how is *not* rigorous, and AI could be the thing that finally tests whether it holds up. Worth deciding whether these are in tension or complementary — probably complementary: Matt's version is earned through genuine exposure; the organizational version is usually inherited without diagnosis.

**"Inside PM at Stripe: What Comes After 'Become a Builder'?"** — Nikhyl Singhal interviews Kevin Yien (Stripe), *The Skip*, July 22, 2026.
*Captured: 2026-07-25 · Source: https://theskip.substack.com/p/inside-pm-at-stripe-what-comes-after*

Relevant threads:
- **Deep generalists.** Kevin's framing of "deep generalist" — earned domain experience combined with refusing to be confined by a narrow job description — as the winning profile now that AI has commoditized raw domain knowledge. Illustrated with a Meta PM whose econ and physics PhDs outperformed classically technical PMs by reasoning from first principles. Strong external validation for **Fence-Shaped, Not T-Shaped**. Kevin's version is career-strategy-scoped; Satsuma's framing extends it into a studio-level thesis about crossing industries, not just crossing functions.
- **Altitude collapse, from the other seat.** Kevin's own story of getting pulled into shipping small fixes with an internal coding agent until a colleague pointed out that anyone could now trigger dozens of agents to fix typos, but only Kevin could make the actual directional call that shapes years of compounding effort. A vivid, concrete illustration of **The Altitude Collapse** thesis, told from inside a real company.
- **Promotion theater.** Kevin's frustration with teams celebrating volume of AI-generated output — prototypes, demos — with no actual user impact or decisions changed behind them. Relevant to **Boring Parts of the Business** and possibly a new angle within **Negative Know-How** — mistaking output volume for judgment.
- **The team brain.** Kevin's argument that the job is shifting from "use the tools to do the work" to "build the system that builds the product" — a shared, judgment-layered knowledge repository that lets a team answer with a glimmer of a leader's judgment even when that person isn't in the room. Independent validation of what Satsuma has already built for itself. Logged as its own piece above: **The Team Brain**.
- **Two audiences.** Kevin's distinction between writing for humans (persuasion — getting people to actually believe something) and writing for agents (precise instruction, close to natural-language pseudocode). Relevant to **Commander's Intent for Product**.
- **Closing line, striking overlap.** The piece closes on the idea that tools stop being the differentiator the moment everyone has them, and the edge becomes the opinion a person forces into the machine. Close to *Why We Build*'s hammer thesis, arrived at independently from a completely different context — a Stripe product organization rather than a solo founder building a venture studio. Worth knowing this exists as independent confirmation, not as something to cite inside the piece itself.

**"Taste Cannot Be Delegated"** — Aurélie Radom, *UX Collective*, July 2026. Part of a three-piece series with "AI made everyone a creator, not a designer" and "The organizational cost of low taste."
*Captured: 2026-07-25 · Source: https://uxdesign.cc/taste-cannot-be-delegated-1706847a0b4b*

Relevant threads:
- **Taste defined from the opposite direction.** Radom: taste comes from exposure to exceptional work — studying history, proportion, typography, composition — until quality is recognizable with consistency. Matt's "negative knowledge base" in *Why We Build* is the mirror image: pattern recognition from exposure to enough failure to recognize a broken pattern before it finishes happening. Same mechanism, opposite direction. Strong material for **The Design Sense Question** — the piece could use both angles rather than just one.
- **The central claim.** "Taste can be assisted. It can be challenged. It can be refined. But it cannot be delegated." Close in spirit to *Why We Build*'s "what isn't commoditized is the structure around the capability." Worth a decision: should **The Design Sense Question** adopt "taste" as its central word now that there's a named essay making nearly the same claim, or keep "negative knowledge base" as Satsuma's own term to avoid sounding derivative?
- **Companion piece: "AI made everyone a creator, not a designer."** Argues a designer's value no longer comes from producing more outputs but from knowing which outputs deserve to exist. A design-specific version of *Why We Build*'s hammer thesis — anyone can swing it now; the differentiator is knowing which nails are actually nails.
- **Companion piece: "The organizational cost of low taste."** On judgment getting diluted across layers of organizational approval — not lost, just diffused until nobody owns it. Relevant to **The Clarity Tax** — organizations that never defined what "good" means end up with taste that exists somewhere in the building but isn't usable by anyone.
